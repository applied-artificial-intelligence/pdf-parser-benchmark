# PDF-Bench Project Orientation

This is the entry point for agents resuming PDF-Bench work in the context of Applied AI's broader document-AI positioning. Read this before changing benchmark results, content, reports, or website exports.

## Current Mission

PDF-Bench should produce a useful practical benchmark story, not a narrow leaderboard and not an audit that discards imperfect evidence. The goal is to show Applied AI competence in PDF/document-intelligence work:

- how hard it is to compare PDF parsers honestly,
- what we tried across business, academic, synthetic, SEC, presentation, commercial, and frontier-parser tracks,
- what each track can and cannot tell us,
- how parser choice changes by document family, metric, cost, and downstream task,
- how PDF Smith and PDF-Bench make parser evaluation operationally manageable.

The intended deliverables are:

- a publishable briefing for technical leaders, engineers, and business readers,
- a meatier technical report with evidence tiers, metrics, result tables, and methodology,
- a cleaner public benchmark/package story that supports Applied AI's sales and positioning.

## What Happened

The project started as an ambitious parser benchmark over business documents with ground truth. That turned out to be hard because public business PDFs rarely come with reliable source or semantic truth.

Work then expanded through several tracks:

- synthetic business documents, including invoices and investor-deck prototypes,
- legal and HR corpora,
- arXiv/academic papers using HTML and later LaTeX-derived ground truth,
- SEC filing PDFs paired with SEC/company HTML,
- presentation/PPTX/PDF attempts,
- commercial parser pilots,
- frontier/foundation-model parser pilots,
- PDF Smith integration as a shared parser abstraction layer.

The history is messy. There are many configs, result artifacts, partial runs, older assumptions, and superseded outputs. The right response is not to throw most of it away. The right response is to preserve it in evidence tiers and use limitations as part of the story.

## Current Framing

The benchmark's central claim is:

> PDF parser benchmarking is difficult because ground truth is scarce, document types fail differently, parser outputs are heterogeneous, metrics disagree, and paid/frontier parsers are easy to misconfigure. PDF-Bench is Applied AI's practical attempt to make useful parser-selection decisions anyway.

Avoid two failure modes:

- Do not overclaim a universal winner from mixed evidence.
- Do not reduce the project to "only two clean tracks matter."

Use evidence tiers:

- Stronger comparison: suitable for direct tables and stronger claims.
- Directional/diagnostic: useful evidence with clear caveats.
- Prototype: pipeline or corpus-building work that is promising but not final.
- Historical/rerun: older runs that explain lessons and may guide future reruns.

## Important Result Signals

Use generated outputs as the source of truth, not memory. Regenerate before important edits.

Current key signals from `data_exports/internal_benchmark_tables.md`:

- SEC filings: top open-source parsers cluster tightly around 0.92-0.93 edit similarity. This suggests clean born-digital reports may not separate top parsers sharply.
- Invoices: Azure Document Intelligence leads the controlled invoice track on edit similarity; parser choice matters more on structured documents.
- Presentations: legacy deck results are useful as stress signals, but not as polished investor-deck proof.
- Investor decks: the source-first pipeline exists and improved through visual iterations, but it is still a prototype.
- arXiv: technically useful stress track, but separate from the business-document story.
- Frontier/commercial pilots: useful for cost/configuration lessons; avoid expensive reruns without a concrete decision.

4. Continue from the active briefing and technical report in `content-marketing/applied-ai/briefings/pdf-parsing/`.
5. Preserve broad project history in tiers. Do not silently delete tracks because they are imperfect.
6. When a track is weak, explain what it still teaches and what would make it stronger.
7. Rerun parsers only when the rerun answers a concrete publication or engineering question.
